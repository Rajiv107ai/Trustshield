## 1. Model Details

### Architecture Overview
TrustShield employs a multi-tiered defense-in-depth architecture:
- **Phase 1 Tabular Baseline:** Random Forest / XGBoost trained on behavioral buyer/seller aggregates and price anomaly ratios.
- **Phase 3 Graph Augmentation:** NetworkX-computed topological metrics (PageRank, shared device/address connected component sizes, degree centrality) fused with tabular features.
- **Phase 4 Multimodal Detector:** Cosine similarity between CLIP text/image embeddings combined with seller age and listing pricing deviation to flag fake/mismatched listings.
- **Phase 5 Hybrid Deep Architecture:** 32-dimensional GNN buyer and seller node embeddings concatenated with Phase 3 feature vectors and scored via gradient boosting.
- **Unified Trust Engine:** Information-theoretic aggregator combining detector outputs into calibrated fraud risk, binary entropy confidence, detector disagreement, reason codes, and operational decision tiers (`ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, `HOLD`, `BLOCK`).

## 2. Intended Use & Application Scope

### Intended Applications
- **Real-Time Transaction Scoring:** Evaluating inbound orders for rapid velocity, account takeover, or synthetic identities at checkout.
- **Fraud Ring Identification:** Identifying coordinated collusion clusters sharing hardware identifiers, IP subnets, or delivery destinations.
- **Listing Integrity Verification:** Detecting counterfeit, fraudulent, or stock-photo mismatched product listings prior to catalog indexing.

### Out-of-Scope & Prohibited Uses
- **Autonomous Credit Scoring:** Not calibrated or legally permissible for credit underwriting or consumer loan decisions.
- **Sole Arbiter for Account Bans:** Transactions categorized as `HOLD` or `REVIEW` must route to trained human fraud analysts. Fully autonomous rejection without human oversight is prohibited.
- **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Model weights and graph topological priors are domain-specific to e-commerce marketplace behaviors.

---

## 3. Training Data & Temporal Split Protocol

### Data Generation
The models are trained and validated on synthetic marketplace transactions generated by the TrustShield simulation engine:
- **Entities:** 1,000 sellers, 10,000 buyers, 5,000 product catalog entries, device logs, address sharing logs.
- **Injected Fraud Scenarios:** Rapid-fire buyer velocity, synthetic identity clustering, buyer-merchant collusion rings, return fraud.

### Chronological Cutoffs (Zero Data Snooping)
- **Training Set:** Orders with `order_date <= TRAIN_END` (Days 1–45).
- **Validation Set:** Orders with `TRAIN_END < order_date <= VAL_END` (Days 46–60). Used strictly for threshold tuning, early stopping, and probability calibration.
- **Test Set:** Orders with `order_date > VAL_END` (Days 61–75). Frozen holdout used solely for final unbiased evaluation reporting.

## 5. Operational Considerations & Guardrails

1. **Cold-Start Entities:** Entities with $< 3$ prior transactions are explicitly flagged (`cold_start = True`). In accordance with epistemic uncertainty principles, their confidence score is attenuated by 30 percent, and zero-vectors replace historical embeddings.
2. **Anti-Feedback Loop Guard:** Model predictions and risk scores are explicitly banned from feature inputs to prevent model echo chambers and distribution drift.
3. **Detector Disagreement:** When component detectors diverge by $> 0.4$ in risk estimation, the transaction is flagged with `HIGH_DETECTOR_DISAGREEMENT` and prioritized for manual review.
4. **Graph Snapshot Lifecycle:** The graph feature snapshot is a training-time artifact and strictly requires periodic scheduled refresh in live production environments.

## 6. Known Limitations & What This Does NOT Show

### What This Does NOT Show

1. **Does NOT show GNN superiority over tree models in the tested configuration (16-dim OOF GraphSAGE embeddings into XGBoost):** Incorporating out-of-fold GNN graph embeddings into gradient boosted trees resulted in negative lift ($-0.0263$ ROC-AUC, $p = 0.0083$). Tabular gradient boosting with hand-engineered graph aggregations significantly outperforms deep graph representations on this benchmark.
2. **Does NOT show double-digit graph lift:** Sharing-based features provide a modest, statistically significant ROC-AUC lift of $\sim +0.011$ ($+0.0114$ on truncated test, $p = 3.02 \times 10^{-5}$; $+0.0115$ on full test, $p = 4.73 \times 10^{-5}$) and $+0.0081$ PR-AUC ($p = 0.0022$) over tabular models that already include device sharing counts. Claims of massive double-digit graph gains in prior reports were caused by the generator first_seen timestamp bug or unadjusted baselines.
3. **Does NOT show significant practical lift at operational review budgets:** At realistic manual review budgets of 2% and 5% of transaction volume, sharing-based features show **no statistically significant lift** over the tabular baseline in either fraud recall (2% budget: $-0.06\%$ lift, $p = 0.3648$; 5% budget: $+0.09\%$ lift, $p = 0.4688$) or fraud monetary value caught (2% budget: $-\text{INR } 792$, $p = 0.9305$; 5% budget: $+\text{INR } 6,943$, $p = 0.2529$).
4. **Does NOT show detection of seller-buyer collusion or low-budget coordinated rings:** `seller_buyer_collusion` has no detectable signal in the current generator/features by construction because 98.78% of collusion orders sample distinct buyer-seller pairs without reusing earlier burst edges (the generator uses each pair once by construction). Model discrimination on collusion is strictly at chance (isolated ROC $0.5001$ baseline vs $0.4971$ graph). Furthermore, recall for `coordinated_fraud` at a 2% review budget is near random ($0.67\%$ baseline vs $0.68\%$ graph), detecting fewer than 1% of coordinated fraud orders.
5. **Does NOT show that a 0.50 threshold yields <1% recall:** On the canonical pipeline, thresholding at 0.50 yields **31.83% ± 3.69% recall** on calibrated probabilities and **40.22% ± 3.30% recall** on raw XGBoost scores. Neither scale yields <1% recall.
6. **Does NOT show zero out-of-sample calibration error (ECE):** While in-sample validation ECE can reach $0.0000$ via isotonic regression, test-set ECE is strictly non-zero ($\sim 0.021 - 0.023$ calibrated, $\sim 0.054 - 0.063$ raw), even though in-sample isotonic validation achieves 0.0000.
7. **Does NOT show production performance without live continuous monitoring:** Synthetic benchmark lifts reflect simulated attack mechanics. Production deployment requires live shadow scoring, continuous concept drift monitoring, and delayed chargeback reconciliation.
